How to read a lead card, and how the picks were made

Pick the lead at team preview, then the turn-one line for what they send out. Percentages are how often to click each line; use any randomizer. The two numbers are your win chance after turn one against how the Showdown ladder clicks, then against a player who reads your mix; red means the lead loses turn one. “Sets Trick Room” is how often the plan clicks it on turn one.

Each card is one group of real teams, filed under the tab of its nearest archetype; the list above says which card a team belongs to. The four on the card won the most whole-game sims when each of the group’s commonest sixes also brought its own best four against it (both sides switching, every turn played out, Trick Room and endgames included). Its lines are the turn-one plan for that lead.

How much Trick Room is worth depends on the archetype; each card uses its tab’s. In the 2v2 endgame sim (your back two against the pairs that archetype has on the field at turn 3, Trick Room up for 2–4 turns or not at all), it changes your win share by: Sand +43; Balance +37; Snow +30; Tailwind +29; Trick Room teams +19; Rain + Sun +18; Sun +16; Perish +13; Rain +12; Indeedee TR -3 points. The lead scores use those ratios, so Trick Room counts more against fast teams and less against slow ones.

The game plan under each lead is how the four was played in whole-game sims against the card’s commonest leads (up to six, 32 games each), weighted by how often each lead appears: the usual turn two, when Trick Room goes up and is set again, when the back row comes in, when Blastoise Mega Evolves, what loses games, and the opposing Pokémon that beat the plan most. It describes the usual game, not a rule. “Adjust the four” comes from the bring matrix: how many points more (green) or fewer (red) bringing one of yours won when their four included that Pokémon, against that card’s average. Seeing it in their six makes it likely they bring it, not certain. Only differences larger than two standard errors and 4 points are listed.

The opponents are real teams: the 8,992 sides with open team sheets in the cached Showdown M-C Bo3 games, split by Battle sim’s ordered core rules (a team takes the first rule its six matches); teams that match no rule make one “other” card per archetype. Each card is modelled on its own teams: its commonest lead pairs (seen at least 5 times, up to six), how they click on turn one and the back rows they bring, and each Pokémon’s commonest set. The four was picked with whole-game sims: full 4v4 battles in a native port of Showdown’s engine, both sides playing each turn’s best mix (switches included) to the end. The 39 fours of yours that made any archetype’s top 10 in the earlier sims played 8 games against every lead of every card; the leaders were re-run on 32 fresh games each; then each card’s top four candidates met the card’s four commonest sixes, each six bringing its strongest four. To find it, every four a six can bring was played against your four (4 games each) and its two strongest re-played 32 games each; the worse of the two counts. A four can only be picked once its own strongest answers have been searched. The turn-one lines were then run with 12 RNG samples per cell in Showdown’s engine with Champions mechanics, the lead’s own back row behind it, and scored with a win model fit on 7,781 games. Two changes for a Trick Room team: Trick Room up after turn one counts for whichever side brought the slower four, scaled by how much slower and by how much Trick Room decided the 2v2 endgames against the card’s archetype (its overall size still comes from the replay fit), not for whoever clicked it, and a Tailwind set in the same turn as Trick Room scores nothing, since it runs out inside Trick Room. Your Indeedee holds Psychic Seed. Their Indeedee F’s item is mixed at replay rates: Rocky Helmet 48%, Colbur Berry 32%, Psychic Seed 20%. Iron Head replaces Kowtow Cleave into Sneasler, because Fighting resists Dark. Their leads outside the card’s commonest are not simulated.

Speed under Trick Room

Where these spreads come from

Lowest moves first. Your six are shaded; the others are the most common opposing spreads on the Showdown ladder (Smogon’s August stats, the latest published) and in tournament sets, with the Speed points each spread uses. Equal numbers are a coin flip.

Nothing common sits at 89, or between 64 and 69. One Speed point on Blastoise (89) only changes the two 88s, Quiet Mega Blastoise and Sylveon’s most common spread: you move before them without Trick Room and after them under it, instead of a coin flip; the usual Mega Blastoise is at 130 either way. Your Brave Kingambit (63) only changes the mirror: under Trick Room it moves before every Adamant Kingambit instead of tying the 70s, and without Trick Room it moves after the 70s instead of tying them. Sylveon’s spreads are scattered from 80 to 103.

Sets. Spreads are the most common ones in Showdown’s Reg M-B Bo3 ladder stats (Smogon, August 2026) and MetaVGC’s tournament and ladder data (Indeedee, Farigiraf, Kingambit, Torkoal). Blastoise’s nature follows the build with exactly its four moves on Pokémon Zone. Incineroar’s set is the most common one on Indeedee Trick Room teams in the 5,000 M-C Bo3 replays the leads were simulated from. The two spare stat points go wherever the top spread put them; they change nothing that matters.

Damage. Coverage and Matchups use an exact damage calculator that follows Showdown’s formula, checked against the Smogon calculator, without crits. The leads were simulated in Showdown’s own engine with Champions mechanics.
