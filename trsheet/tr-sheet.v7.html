Leads

Pick the lead at team preview, then the turn-one line for what they send out. Percentages are how often to click each line; use any randomizer. The two numbers are your win chance after turn one against how the Showdown ladder clicks, then against a player who reads your mix; red means the lead loses turn one. “Sets Trick Room” is how often the plan clicks it on turn one.

The first card is the main lead. That is Farigiraf + Blastoise wherever it comes within 3 points of the strongest lead, which is every tab except Tailwind and Trick Room teams with a Farigiraf; the strongest lead follows it, marked “Top lead”.

A lead marked If Trick Room counts double is the best one when the win model’s bonus for having Trick Room up after turn one (about 15 points of win chance from an even spot) is doubled. That bonus was fit on every team that sets Trick Room, and a team built entirely around it probably gets more from it. Its numbers are still scored with the fitted model, so they compare directly with the leads above it. It only appears against archetypes whose leads almost never set Trick Room themselves (under 5% of the time on turn one), since their own Trick Room makes yours worth less, not more.

The six

Each set uses the most common spread for that Pokémon running these moves. Stat points (SP) are what you assign in the game; the stat row is the result at level 50, after Mega Evolution for Blastoise.

Bringing four: two setters and two attackers works for most games, and each lead in the Leads section names a back row. Keep the second setter in the back: it replaces a setter that falls and resets Trick Room when it ends.

Speed under Trick Room

Lowest moves first. Your six are shaded; the others are the most common opposing spreads on the Showdown ladder (Smogon’s August stats, the latest published) and in tournament sets, with the Speed points each spread uses. Equal numbers are a coin flip.

Nothing common sits at 89, or between 64 and 69. One Speed point on Blastoise (89) only changes the two 88s, Quiet Mega Blastoise and Sylveon’s most common spread: you move before them without Trick Room and after them under it, instead of a coin flip; the usual Mega Blastoise is at 130 either way. Your Brave Kingambit (63) only changes the mirror: under Trick Room it moves before every Adamant Kingambit instead of tying the 70s, and without Trick Room it moves after the 70s instead of tying them. Sylveon’s spreads are scattered from 80 to 103.

Sets. Spreads are the most common ones in Showdown’s Reg M-B Bo3 ladder stats (Smogon, August 2026) and MetaVGC’s tournament and ladder data (Hatterene, Indeedee, Farigiraf, Kingambit, Torkoal). Blastoise’s nature follows the build with exactly its four moves on Pokémon Zone. The two spare stat points go wherever the top spread put them; they change nothing that matters.
